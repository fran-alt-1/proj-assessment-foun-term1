PAGE 05 · CAREERS & CERTIFICATIONS

People build cybersecurity careers in different ways

A degree, hands-on practice, communication skills and recognised learning can all help. Job titles differ between employers, so read the actual role description.

01

Security Operations Centre (SOC) Analyst

What they do: review security alerts, look for suspicious patterns, document findings and pass incidents to the right responders.

Useful skills: attention to detail, basic networking, log analysis and clear communication.

Learning path: networking basics → security fundamentals → authorised practice labs → junior analyst applications.

02

Cybersecurity Technician

What they do: help configure and maintain protective tools, support users and follow secure procedures for devices and accounts.

Useful skills: troubleshooting, operating-system basics, account security and customer support.

Learning path: IT support foundations → security essentials → supervised hands-on experience.

03

Incident Response Analyst

What they do: help investigate security events, limit their effect, preserve useful records and support recovery.

Useful skills: calm communication, careful documentation, analytical thinking and knowledge of incident procedures.

Learning path: IT and network foundations → security operations → incident-response practice in authorised settings.

SKILLS THAT TRAVEL

Practice the skills behind the tools.

Cybersecurity workers need to explain risk clearly, follow ethical boundaries and work with people across an organisation. Good habits include documenting decisions, asking when authority is unclear and protecting information encountered during work.

  • Read a job listing and highlight repeated skills.
  • Learn basic networks and operating systems.
  • Build a small portfolio of safe, authorised defensive projects.
  • Keep learning as tools and threats change.
